---
title: What is your Goal in Life?
date: 2026-10-24
summary: maybe life is just an impossible optimisation question
---

recently, too many people have been asking me *"what is your goal in life?"* and, 
for a question that gets thrown around so often,
i've never seemed to have a good answer.

looking at the people around me, there seem to be plenty of answers. 
some people want to hillclimb on everything they do, 
some want to have the most fun possible, 
some want to escape the permanent underclass, et cetera.

i think these all roughly point towards the same goal:

> *to live the **best** life possible*

people just have different ideas of what makes a life good, and different measures to help define it.

however, maximising individual metrics like happiness or material wealth to measure the *quality* of life doesn't necessarily get you there; 
it's eventually counter-productive, often not reflecting what you really care about (Goodhart's Law).

following that train of thought, it makes sense that at each point in life you have a varied set of priorities but a finite amount of resources to allocate to each priority.
how much each priority adds to your life depends on how much you put into it, what you’re giving up, and how it affects everything else.

therefore, assuming that you have a perfect model on your current state of life, with weights that accurately depict relationships between priorities,
perhaps you could identify the Pareto frontier of possible resource allocations: the allocations where improving one priority would mean sacrificing another. 
each allocation would bring a different combination of benefits to your life, and you could use those weights to decide which combination you preferred.

perhaps the *best life* would reveal itself somewhere in that process.

however, this assumes i can meaningfully anticipate what i will value, how my choices will affect it, and that i am able to weigh my present priorities against my future ones. 

how can i build that model when part of living itself is discovering what i want?

maybe, it's okay to leave this question unanswered.
